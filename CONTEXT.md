# Zammy

A news aggregator. Users search keywords and get a time-ordered timeline of headlines;
a landing-page cloud shows what the day's news is mostly about. Content comes from the
Currents API, which re-emits the same story many times over — a quirk that shapes most
of the language below — and optionally from GNews alongside it.

## Language

### Content

**Sighting**:
One emission of an article by an upstream API. Upstream re-emits the same story under
fresh identifiers — one page arrived 50 times in 100 results — so a sighting is an
observation, not a story. Two providers reporting one URL is likewise two sightings of
one article, not a duplicate to resolve.
_Avoid_: emission, raw article, row

**Provider**:
An upstream news API we fetch from — Currents, and optionally GNews. Providers are not
equals: one is **coverage-bearing** and alone decides when a time window has been fully
fetched, while any other is best-effort breadth whose absence is never recorded as
coverage (ADR-0012). Distinct from **source**, which is the outlet that published an
article; one provider reports many sources, and two providers report many of the same.
_Avoid_: upstream (as a countable noun), API, feed, source

**Article**:
A single real-world news story, identified by its canonical URL. An article has one or
more sightings. This is the unit users see and the unit anything countable counts.
_Avoid_: cluster, story, item, post

**Group**:
One story as several outlets ran it: the same piece syndicated at different URLs, kept as separate
articles and joined rather than merged. Groups attach to storylines; a timeline slot is one
storyline's coverage on one day. Most groups hold a single article, and a group never spans two
calendar days.
_Avoid_: cluster, duplicate, merge, story

**Storyline**:
One specific event or evolving situation — "Trump Iran War", never bare "Donald Trump" — as a
persistent collection of groups, identified by UUID and unbounded in time: an ongoing story holds
its whole arc. Paged by day regardless: each day it has coverage it takes one timeline slot holding
that day's articles, its earliest as the hero. A group may belong to several storylines. Every
group is born a provisional storyline of one, invisible as such; a second group's attachment
promotes it. Formation errs narrow, and storylines merge automatically — either bridged (K
independent groups qualifying for both) or label-agreement (two model labels agreeing, grounded,
spans overlapping or near-touching) — the loser's UUID kept as an alias.
_Avoid_: cluster, thread, saga, topic

**Representative**:
The article whose headline a timeline slot shows. Chosen per topic from the slot's lead group —
the largest the storyline shows that day — among the members that matched that search, by source
authority; never by how often upstream re-emitted it.
_Avoid_: primary, canonical, best, winner

**Seed**:
The article a group started from, and its identity — what `group_id` names. Fixed for the group's
life, but not privileged when matching: a newcomer is compared against any member, and what bounds a
group's drift is the calendar day. The seed is neither the representative nor necessarily the group's
earliest article.
_Avoid_: parent, root, head

**Source**:
The outlet that published an article, identified by its registrable domain. Two sources
covering the same story yield two articles, deliberately.
_Avoid_: publisher, feed, provider

### Discovery

**Topic**:
A canonicalized search — keywords plus optional language, country and category — that
accumulates articles over time and is shared by everyone who searches the same thing.
_Avoid_: search, query, feed, tag

**Keyword**:
A word a user types in. Keywords are input to a topic.
_Avoid_: term, tag, phrase

**Term**:
A proper noun, or a two-word phrase containing one, extracted from article headlines and ranked
for the landing-page cloud. Generic words never qualify, however frequent — alone or paired — and
neither does anything containing a verb: a term names something, it does not state something.
Terms are output, keywords are input; clicking a term opens a topic. A term is a surface form,
not a concept: "China" and "Chinese" are two terms and both may appear.
_Avoid_: topic, keyword, trend, tag

**Fingerprint**:
The stable identity of a topic, derived from its canonicalized keywords and filters and
deliberately excluding any time window — so the same search always finds the same topic.
_Avoid_: hash, key, topic id

**Coverage**:
The time spans for which a topic's articles have actually been fetched. Coverage is what
separates "nothing was published then" from "we never asked".
_Avoid_: history, range, window

**Storyline label**:
Two to five words naming a storyline at a glance — a proper noun plus what happened: "Fort Hood
Crash". Generated by an LLM when a storyline is promoted and regenerated on merge or drift.
Presentation, not identity: labels are deliberately non-unique, two storylines may share one and
are told apart by date span, and clicking a label opens the storyline itself by UUID — its words
are never executed as a search. But agreement between two labels is merge evidence: storylines
independently given the same grounded label, with overlapping or near-touching spans, merge on
that agreement alone, since a big running story can outgrow what the token bridge can reach. Not a
term: a term earns a cloud slot by rank; a label is already anchored by the storyline it names.
_Avoid_: term, keyword, phrase, tag, title
