# Storyline labels — what the model costs, and what the floor looks like

Measured record for storylines-v2 S4 (ADR-0011, "Labels"). Numbers below come
from the dev archive on 2026-08-14: 22,987 articles spanning 2026-07-14 →
2026-08-14, `APP_TIMEZONE=UTC`. Reproduce with:

```bash
pnpm label:storylines --preview --limit=20
```

The model numbers come from a live run against `gemini-3.1-flash-lite` on the
same date. Nothing in the test suite reaches the live API; the run below is
what the recorded fixtures were checked against.

## What a label is

Two to five words naming a storyline at a glance — a proper noun plus what
happened: "Fort Hood Crash". Generated by Gemini's flash tier at promotion, on
merge, and on drift; validated against ADR-0011's contract; falling back to the
bigram extractor and then to nothing. Presentation, never identity: nothing
keys off a label, two storylines may share one, and a null label renders.

That last clause is the whole reason an external model is tolerable here. The
worst failure this dependency can produce is a storyline with no name.

## The three triggers

| trigger | who decides | rule |
| --- | --- | --- |
| promotion | the linker (S3) | a second group attached; the storyline just became real |
| merge | the linker (S3) | two storylines became one, so the story changed shape |
| drift | `needsLabel` | member groups ≥ `label_group_count × STORYLINE_LABEL_DRIFT_FACTOR` (default 2) |

Promotion and merge are events a caller passes in. Drift is the only one this
layer decides, and it needs `storylines.label_group_count` (migration 0010) —
which cannot be derived, because `storyline_groups.attached_at` survives a
merge and so does not say when a group reached *this* storyline.

The baseline is stamped even when the label comes out null. Otherwise a
storyline nobody can name is re-asked on every pass for ever, which turns a
storyline-volume cost into an unbounded one.

## Choosing the model, measured

Three findings, none of which survived contact with a guess:

**`gemini-2.5-flash` 404s.** It is still listed under `/models` and refuses on
use — "no longer available to new users". A model id that appears in the
catalogue and fails on call is exactly why this is config and not a constant.

**`maxOutputTokens` counts thinking tokens.** The cap started at 32, on the
reasoning that a label is three words. Against `gemini-3.7-flash` that returned
an EMPTY candidate with `finishReason: MAX_TOKENS` — the entire budget spent on
thoughts before a word was written. The cap is now 256, which costs nothing
unless used.

**Thinking buys nothing here and bills as output.** Asked to name the Fort Hood
crash:

| model | thinkingConfig | thoughts | answer |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| gemini-3.7-flash | omitted | 463 | Fort Hood Crash |
| gemini-3.7-flash | `low` (its floor; `none`/`minimal` are 400s) | 300 | Fort Hood Crash |
| gemini-3.5-flash | `low` | 344 | Fort Hood crash |
| gemini-3.1-flash-lite | `low` | 121 | Fort Hood Crash |
| **gemini-3.1-flash-lite** | **omitted** | **0** | **Fort Hood Crash** |

Naming a story is recall, not reasoning. Flash-lite with the field omitted is
the default: same answer, no thinking tokens, cheapest tier. `GEMINI_MODEL` and
`GEMINI_THINKING_LEVEL` remain knobs because the families spell this
differently and a later model may need it.

## Cost

Measured over 20 real day-bounded storylines from the archive: **712 input tokens per label**
(10 member headlines, descriptions trimmed to 200 chars), plus ~4 output tokens
and — at the default settings — no thinking tokens at all.

Per 1,000 labels: ~0.71M input, ~0.004M output. At the flash-tier rates
published at the time of writing — $0.30/M input, $2.50/M output — that is

    0.71M × $0.30  +  0.004M × $2.50  ≈  $0.22 per 1,000 labels

Had the thinking finding gone unnoticed, `gemini-3.7-flash` at `low` would have
added ~0.3M output tokens per thousand — ~$0.75, quadrupling the bill for an
identical answer.

The rates are the part most likely to be stale; the token counts are measured
and will not be. Even a tenfold error in either direction leaves this a
rounding error, which is the only property the decision actually rests on.

Volume is small by construction. The archive holds 3,437 multi-group
day-bounded storylines over 32 days — ~107/day — and that is a generous CEILING
for v2 promotions, because a v2 storyline persists and is promoted once, where
the old day-bounded model re-formed its storylines every day. At ~110 labels/day the bill is around
**$0.02/day**. Labels run at storyline volume; the linker runs at group volume
(~1,200/day) and is deliberately not a model call (ADR-0011, "Considered and
refused").

## What the model produces, measured

20 day-bounded storylines, live, at the defaults above: **17 model labels, 3
fallbacks, 0 unnamed.** Against the same storylines the extractor alone
produced:

| model | bigram fallback |
| --- | --- |
| Trump Executive Orders | Executive Order |
| Hormuz Strait Crisis | Strait Hormuz |
| Ukraine Russia Escalation | Ballistic Missile |
| Trump Netanyahu Summit | Israeli Prime |
| Gaza Disarmament Standoff | Gaza Hamas |
| Europe Wildfire Crisis | France Spain |
| USS Lincoln Crisis | Uss Abraham |
| AI Memory Race | Ai Memory |
| Canada US Tensions | Social Media |

The left column names events. The right names entities, or nothing. That gap is
ADR-0011's justification for the model, reproduced on live output rather than
argued.

Three qualifications, all honest:

- **Three failures in twenty**: one 503 (retried, still 503), one lost to the
  8s deadline — now 20s — and one contract refusal, "Osun Election 2026",
  refused for a digit on a rule ADR-0011 never wrote. Two of the three are
  fixed; the 503 is what the fallback is for.
- **Garbage in, plausible out.** "Market Trends August" and "Global Weather
  Events" came from day-bounded storylines the OLD model had swallowed —
  68 and 33 unrelated groups. They pass validation ("August" is capitalized
  and grounded) and name nothing. Grounding cannot catch this; a real v2
  storyline, formed err-narrow, is a different input.
- **One label looked invented and was not.** "Ceuta Migrant Crisis" was
  produced for a day-bounded storyline whose newest headline is about
  Netanyahu — but Ceuta appears in 5 of its 12 headlines, alongside RBI, Rahul
  Gandhi and Nigel Farage. The model found the plurality story inside a
  swallowed day-bounded storyline.

Casing is not normalized on model output ("Infantino leadership crisis" came
back part-lowercase). Forcing Title Case would break names that carry their own
case, and this is presentation that regenerates; left alone deliberately.

## The fallback floor, measured

With no `GEMINI_API_KEY` the preview labels **30 of 30** day-bounded storylines from the
bigram extractor. Freeing it from ADR-0010's containment rule fixed the yield
problem outright — the old rule produced 9 phrases from 46 eligible slots — but
yield was never the hard part. A sample of what it produces:

| label | what it is |
| --- | --- |
| Karoline Leavitt | a person, not an event |
| Strait Hormuz | a place, not an event |
| Berkshire Hathaway | a company, not an event |
| Social Media, Price Target, Wall Street, World Cup | generic pairs |
| Ai Help, Ai Memory, Hit High, Tens Thousands, Investing Com | not names of anything |

Roughly two thirds contain a proper noun and almost none name a happening. This
is ADR-0011's finding reproduced rather than merely quoted: the extractor
cannot reach "names the event at a glance", and that gap is the entire
justification for the model. It is the floor — what stands between an
unavailable Gemini and a nameless storyline — not the target.

(The day-bounded storylines above come from the OLD model, borrowed by
`--preview` as a source of real multi-article coverage because the v2 linker is
S3 and does not exist yet. Some are visibly swallowed — 76 groups under
"Executive Order" — which is ADR-0009's measured failure, not the labeler's.)

## What is validated, and what is not

`validateLabel` enforces three of the contract's four clauses:

- **2 to 5 words**, after stripping the quotes and emphasis a model wraps an
  answer in — but not the apostrophe inside Xi'an, which is part of the name.
- **No punctuation**: letters, digits, spaces, and the hyphen/apostrophe that
  live inside names. A sentence fails here, which is how a chatty answer is
  caught. Digits are admitted on live evidence — see "Osun Election 2026"
  above; the contract bars punctuation and a year is not punctuation.
- **Contains a proper noun**, enforced as GROUNDING: at least one capitalized
  word must appear in the coverage it names, under the same singular fold the
  rest of the system uses. Corpus case evidence (`properNounEvidence`) was the
  alternative and is too strict — it is conservative by design and headlines
  are Title Case, so a storyline whose members carry no descriptions would fail
  every label. Grounding catches the failure that matters: a name that appears
  nowhere in the story.

The fourth clause — **names the event, not just the entity** — is not
enforced, because it is not checkable. "Fort Hood Crash" and "Donald Trump" are
both two capitalized grounded words and no test over tokens separates them. The
prompt carries that clause and live output is what will judge it; ADR-0011 says
the prompt iterates and the contract does not. `storyline-label.test.ts` states
this as a passing test rather than leaving it to be found as a bug.

## The label as merge evidence

A label is presentation, not identity — but two labels *agreeing* turned out to
be the only usable evidence for a merge the token rules cannot reach. ADR-0011's
"Labels" section carries the amendment; this is what the archive said.

**The problem.** The merge is the only cure ADR-0011 gives err-narrow formation,
and it needs a group that straddles two arcs. That fails exactly where it is
needed most. On 2026-08-17 the archive held the Strait of Hormuz coverage as
**25 live arcs** whose entire shared vocabulary was `{hormuz, strait, iran}`:

| token | df | share of a 17,028-article fortnight | anchor ceiling |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `hormuz` | 422 | 2.5% | 34 (0.2%) |
| `strait` | 363 | 2.1% | 34 |
| `iran` | 703 | 4.1% | 34 |

Mass 10.7 against a floor of 20, and not one token able to anchor. No group can
bridge them, so no bridge is recorded and nothing merges. The story's own name
had outgrown the rule meant to recognize it.

**The labeler had already solved it.** Six of those arcs were independently given
the identical name "Strait of Hormuz Crisis".

**What agreement alone would have cost.** The same archive also gave three arcs
the label "Q2 2026 Financial Results" — CVB Financial, Mitsui, Mayfair Gold,
Westaim, unrelated companies reporting earnings. Their spans overlap, so ADR-0010's
date-span disambiguator does not separate them. This is the "garbage in, plausible
out" failure recorded above, arriving where it can do damage.

**What separates them.** `properNounEvidence` — the corpus-case test this document
rejected for label *validation* as too strict:

| label | words with proper-noun evidence | verdict |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Strait of Hormuz Crisis | `hormuz` | merge |
| Q2 2026 Financial Results | none | refused |

"Hormuz" is never written lowercase in the coverage; "financial", "results" and
"quarter" always are. The conservatism that made this test wrong for validation
makes it right here: a refused merge leaves two storylines, which is the status
quo, where a refused *label* leaves a storyline nameless.

**The rule**, then — all four clauses, each refusing something the others cannot
see:

1. Both labels from the **model**. The bigram fallback named sixteen unrelated
   arcs "Strait Hormuz"; that is agreement about vocabulary, which is the anchor
   rule's business.
2. Labels **equal** after casefolding, whitespace collapse and the `singular`
   fold. Stopwords are deliberately *not* elided.
3. The label contains a **grounded proper noun**, pooled over the cluster's whole
   coverage.
4. Spans **overlap or fall within `STORYLINE_LABEL_MERGE_GRACE_DAYS`**.

Transitive within one label and nowhere else: three arcs chaining A–C–B collapse
together, because C covers the gap and all three carry one name. The label is the
boundary, which is what makes this bounded where ADR-0009's transitive token
linking was not.

**Measured on the dev archive** (2026-08-17, 258 model-labelled live storylines):

| label | arcs | grounded names | outcome |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Strait of Hormuz Crisis | 6 | `hormuz` | merged into one 31-group arc |
| Q2 2026 Financial Results | 3 | none | **refused** |

Two shared labels, one merge, one refusal — and the refusal is the load-bearing
half. Yield is small here only because most of the archive is unlabelled (13,390
of 13,648 live storylines have no model label); under the nightly label chore every
promoted storyline carries one.

`STORYLINE_LABEL_MERGE_GRACE_DAYS` is **unmeasured**: every arc of the one
qualifying cluster overlaps every other, so 0 and 30 plan the identical merge. It
needs an archive holding a story that stopped and restarted.

## Operating it

```bash
pnpm label:storylines                  # label promoted storylines that need it
pnpm label:storylines --dry-run        # say which, write nothing
pnpm label:storylines --preview        # read-only quality/cost harness (dies in S9)
pnpm merge:storyline-labels --dry-run  # what the label-agreement merge would do
pnpm merge:storyline-labels            # merge it, and re-name the survivors
```

The dry run prints every shared label with its grounded names and every arc's
span, then the plan and the refusals. Merging has no inverse (ADR-0011 refuses
split), so reading that output is the check — and the refusals are where a
generic model label shows itself.

With no `GEMINI_API_KEY` every path still runs; the model is simply never asked
and `source` reads `bigram` throughout. Tests never reach the live API: the
client is exercised against recorded response payloads in
`fixtures/gemini-label-responses.json`, every shape of which was confirmed
against the real endpoint during the run above.

Latency: ~8s per call end to end over the 20-label run, which is why the
deadline is 20s and why labelling is a background pass and never on a reader's
path.
