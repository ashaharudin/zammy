# Storyline labels — what the model costs, and what the floor looks like

Measured record for storylines-v2 S4 (ADR-0011, "Labels"). Numbers from the dev
archive on 2026-08-14: 22,987 articles spanning 2026-07-14 → 2026-08-14,
`APP_TIMEZONE=UTC`. Model numbers are from a live run against
`gemini-3.1-flash-lite` the same date; nothing in the test suite reaches the
live API. Reproduce with:

```bash
pnpm label:storylines --preview --limit=20
```

## What a label is

Two to five words naming a storyline at a glance — a proper noun plus what
happened: "Fort Hood Crash". Generated by Gemini's flash tier at promotion, on
merge, and on drift; validated against ADR-0011's contract; falling back to the
bigram extractor and then to nothing. Presentation, never identity: nothing
keys off a label, two storylines may share one, and a null label renders. That
last clause is why an external model is tolerable here — the worst failure it
can produce is a storyline with no name.

## The three triggers

| trigger | who decides | rule |
| --- | --- | --- |
| promotion | the linker (S3) | a second group attached; the storyline just became real |
| merge | the linker (S3) | two storylines became one, so the story changed shape |
| drift | `needsLabel` | member groups ≥ `label_group_count × STORYLINE_LABEL_DRIFT_FACTOR` (default 2) |

Promotion and merge are events a caller passes in. Drift is the only one this
layer decides, and it needs `storylines.label_group_count` (migration 0010) —
which cannot be derived, because `storyline_groups.attached_at` survives a
merge and so does not say when a group reached *this* storyline. The baseline
is stamped even when the label comes out null; otherwise a storyline nobody can
name is re-asked on every pass for ever, turning a storyline-volume cost into
an unbounded one.

## Choosing the model, measured

Three findings, none of which survived contact with a guess:

**`gemini-2.5-flash` 404s.** Still listed under `/models`, refuses on use ("no
longer available to new users") — exactly why the model id is config, not a
constant.

**`maxOutputTokens` counts thinking tokens.** The cap started at 32 (a label is
three words); against `gemini-3.7-flash` that returned an EMPTY candidate with
`finishReason: MAX_TOKENS`, the entire budget spent on thoughts. Now 256, which
costs nothing unless used.

**Thinking buys nothing here and bills as output.** Asked to name the Fort Hood
crash:

| model | thinkingConfig | thoughts | answer |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| gemini-3.7-flash | omitted | 463 | Fort Hood Crash |
| gemini-3.7-flash | `low` (its floor; `none`/`minimal` are 400s) | 300 | Fort Hood Crash |
| gemini-3.5-flash | `low` | 344 | Fort Hood crash |
| gemini-3.1-flash-lite | `low` | 121 | Fort Hood Crash |
| **gemini-3.1-flash-lite** | **omitted** | **0** | **Fort Hood Crash** |

Naming a story is recall, not reasoning. Flash-lite with the field omitted is
the default: same answer, no thinking tokens, cheapest tier. `GEMINI_MODEL` and
`GEMINI_THINKING_LEVEL` stay knobs because the families spell this differently
and a later model may need it.

## Cost

Measured over 20 real day-bounded storylines: **712 input tokens per label**
(10 member headlines, descriptions trimmed to 200 chars), ~4 output tokens, no
thinking tokens at the defaults. Per 1,000 labels: ~0.71M input, ~0.004M
output. At the flash-tier rates published at the time of writing — $0.30/M
input, $2.50/M output —

    0.71M × $0.30  +  0.004M × $2.50  ≈  $0.22 per 1,000 labels

Unnoticed, `gemini-3.7-flash` at `low` would have added ~0.3M output tokens per
thousand — ~$0.75, quadrupling the bill for an identical answer. The rates may
go stale; the token counts are measured. Even a tenfold error either way leaves
this a rounding error, the only property the decision rests on.

Volume is small by construction: 3,437 multi-group day-bounded storylines over
32 days (~107/day) is a generous CEILING for v2 promotions, since a v2
storyline persists and is promoted once where the old day-bounded model
re-formed daily. At ~110 labels/day the bill is around **$0.02/day**. Labels
run at storyline volume; the linker runs at group volume (~1,200/day) and is
deliberately not a model call (ADR-0011, "Considered and refused").

## The rate limit, and where it is enforced

The free tier publishes **15 requests per minute**, and the ceiling is on the
KEY — which is why the pacer lives in `gemini.ts`, not in the callers. Five
things label storylines:

| caller | where it runs | when |
| --- | --- | --- |
| the ingest tail (`labelLinkedStorylines`) | the server | promotion and merge, off the §6.3 deadline |
| the late-candidate sweep | `pnpm chores storylines` | nightly, before the drift pass |
| the drift chore (`labelPass`) | `pnpm chores storylines` | nightly |
| `pnpm backfill:storylines` | its own process | an operator, on the whole archive |
| `pnpm label:storylines` | its own process | an operator, on what needs it |

Before this they were paced separately or not at all: the two nightly passes
spaced at 60/min (four times the ceiling), the ingest tail and sweep had no
spacing, and retries were invisible to all four — a bad minute at the API
multiplied the real rate by up to 3. One pacer inside the client fixes the
category of bug rather than four instances of it.

**The pacer's scope is one client, so one process.** The server's ingest tail
uses the instance built in `server.ts`; the chore runner and each CLI build
their own from `wiring.createLabeler`. Two of them running at once is two
pacers each admitting `GEMINI_MAX_RPM` — up to 2x the ceiling (3x with three);
the 429 path below is what holds the key then. The nightly runner is the case
nobody chooses — 04:00 UTC arrives whether or not the server is labelling — so
it takes `--label-rate` like the backfill (STORYLINE-BACKFILL.md, "Operating
it"). Note that `--max-labels` bounds only the drift pass: the sweeps relabel
whatever they merge or attach, and that spend is bounded by what moved.

It is a sliding 60-second window, not a per-minute counter — a counter
resetting on the minute admits a full limit at :59 and another at :01 — and
slots are reserved before the wait, so two callers arriving in the same tick
stack instead of both reading a window with room in it.

Three properties worth knowing:

- **A 429 holds the whole client back.** Earning one despite the pacer means
  something else is on the key, so `Retry-After` becomes a cooldown every
  waiter respects — including waiters ALREADY ASLEEP when it lands: a waiter
  re-reads the cooldown on waking and sleeps again if it moved, because one
  that only checked before sleeping would wake inside the cooldown and send the
  request it exists to stop. Cost: held waiters resume together at the
  cooldown's end rather than re-spacing; the window paces everything after
  them.
- **The queue is bounded.** Past `GEMINI_MAX_RATE_WAIT_MS` (2 minutes, ~30 deep
  at 15/min) a call gives up and takes the bigram fallback — the same move as
  for a 503. Otherwise the ingest tail would grow an unbounded queue during a
  promotion burst, each waiter holding its members in memory. Cost: a sticky
  bigram label — the baseline is stamped either way, so drift will not re-ask
  until membership doubles; `--relabel` is the repair. The CLIs raise the cap
  to 15 minutes, since a backfill would rather wait than settle for a bigram.
- **Serially, latency binds before the limit does.** At ~7.1s per call a single
  pass sustains ~8.5 labels/min, under 15. The ceiling only binds on a
  parallelised pass — exactly what STORYLINE-BACKFILL.md recommends for a full
  relabel, and why the pacer has to be in the client such a pass would share.

`GEMINI_MAX_RPM` is the knob; raise it with the plan and every path above
follows, including the nightly passes, which take it as their spacing.

## What the model produces, measured

20 day-bounded storylines, live, at the defaults: **17 model labels, 3
fallbacks, 0 unnamed.** Against the same storylines, the extractor alone:

| model | bigram fallback |
| --- | --- |
| Trump Executive Orders | Executive Order |
| Hormuz Strait Crisis | Strait Hormuz |
| Ukraine Russia Escalation | Ballistic Missile |
| Trump Netanyahu Summit | Israeli Prime |
| Gaza Disarmament Standoff | Gaza Hamas |
| Europe Wildfire Crisis | France Spain |
| USS Lincoln Crisis | Uss Abraham |
| AI Memory Race | Ai Memory |
| Canada US Tensions | Social Media |

The left column names events; the right names entities, or nothing — ADR-0011's
justification for the model, reproduced on live output.

Three qualifications, all honest:

- **Three failures in twenty**: one 503 (retried, still 503), one lost to the
  8s deadline — now 20s — and one contract refusal, "Osun Election 2026",
  refused for a digit on a rule ADR-0011 never wrote. Two of three fixed; the
  503 is what the fallback is for.
- **Garbage in, plausible out.** "Market Trends August" and "Global Weather
  Events" came from day-bounded storylines the OLD model had swallowed — 68 and
  33 unrelated groups. They pass validation ("August" is capitalized and
  grounded) and name nothing. Grounding cannot catch this; a real v2 storyline,
  formed err-narrow, is a different input.
- **One label looked invented and was not.** "Ceuta Migrant Crisis" named a
  day-bounded storyline whose newest headline is about Netanyahu — but Ceuta
  appears in 5 of its 12 headlines: the model found the plurality story inside
  a swallowed storyline.

Casing is not normalized on model output ("Infantino leadership crisis" came
back part-lowercase): forcing Title Case would break names that carry their own
case, and this is presentation that regenerates. Left alone deliberately.

## The fallback floor, measured

With no `GEMINI_API_KEY` the preview labels **30 of 30** day-bounded storylines
from the bigram extractor. Freeing it from ADR-0010's containment rule fixed
the yield problem outright — the old rule produced 9 phrases from 46 eligible
slots — but yield was never the hard part. A sample:

| label | what it is |
| --- | --- |
| Karoline Leavitt | a person, not an event |
| Strait Hormuz | a place, not an event |
| Berkshire Hathaway | a company, not an event |
| Social Media, Price Target, Wall Street, World Cup | generic pairs |
| Ai Help, Ai Memory, Hit High, Tens Thousands, Investing Com | not names of anything |

Roughly two thirds contain a proper noun and almost none name a happening: the
extractor cannot reach "names the event at a glance", and that gap is the
justification for the model. It is the floor, not the target.

(The day-bounded storylines come from the OLD model, borrowed by `--preview`
because the v2 linker did not exist yet. Some are visibly swallowed — 76 groups
under "Executive Order" — ADR-0009's measured failure, not the labeler's.)

## What is validated, and what is not

`validateLabel` enforces three of the contract's four clauses:

- **2 to 5 words**, after stripping the quotes and emphasis a model wraps an
  answer in — but not the apostrophe inside Xi'an, part of the name.
- **No punctuation**: letters, digits, spaces, and the hyphen/apostrophe that
  live inside names. A sentence fails here, which is how a chatty answer is
  caught. Digits are admitted on live evidence ("Osun Election 2026"): the
  contract bars punctuation and a year is not punctuation.
- **Contains a proper noun**, enforced as GROUNDING: at least one capitalized
  word must appear in the coverage it names, under the same singular fold the
  rest of the system uses. Corpus case evidence (`properNounEvidence`) was the
  alternative and is too strict for validation — conservative by design, and
  headlines are Title Case, so a storyline whose members carry no descriptions
  would fail every label. Grounding catches the failure that matters: a name
  appearing nowhere in the story.

The fourth clause — **names the event, not just the entity** — is not enforced
because it is not checkable: "Fort Hood Crash" and "Donald Trump" are both two
capitalized grounded words, and no test over tokens separates them. The prompt
carries the clause and live output judges it; ADR-0011 says the prompt iterates
and the contract does not. `storyline-label.test.ts` states this as a passing
test rather than leaving it to be found as a bug.

## The label as merge evidence

A label is presentation, not identity — but two labels *agreeing* turned out to
be the only usable evidence for a merge the token rules cannot reach.
ADR-0011's "Labels" section carries the amendment; this is what the archive
said.

**The problem.** The merge is ADR-0011's only cure for err-narrow formation,
and it needs a group that straddles two arcs — which fails exactly where it is
needed most. On 2026-08-17 the archive held the Strait of Hormuz coverage as
**25 live arcs** whose entire shared vocabulary was `{hormuz, strait, iran}`
(linker-side analysis: docs/STORYLINE-LINKING.md, "The anchor ceiling cannot
reach a big running story"):

| token | df | share of a 17,028-article fortnight | anchor ceiling |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `hormuz` | 422 | 2.5% | 34 (0.2%) |
| `strait` | 363 | 2.1% | 34 |
| `iran` | 703 | 4.1% | 34 |

Mass 10.7 against a floor of 20, not one token able to anchor: no group can
bridge them, nothing merges. The story's own name had outgrown the rule meant
to recognize it. **The labeler had already solved it**: six of those arcs were
independently given the identical name "Strait of Hormuz Crisis".

**What agreement alone would have cost.** The same archive gave three arcs the
label "Q2 2026 Financial Results" — CVB Financial, Mitsui, Mayfair Gold,
Westaim, unrelated companies reporting earnings. Their spans overlap, so
ADR-0010's date-span disambiguator does not separate them: the "garbage in,
plausible out" failure above, arriving where it can do damage.

**What separates them.** `properNounEvidence` — the corpus-case test rejected
above for label *validation* as too strict:

| label | words with proper-noun evidence | verdict |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Strait of Hormuz Crisis | `hormuz` | merge |
| Q2 2026 Financial Results | none | refused |

"Hormuz" is never written lowercase in the coverage; "financial", "results" and
"quarter" always are. The conservatism wrong for validation is right here: a
refused merge leaves two storylines — the status quo — where a refused *label*
leaves a storyline nameless.

**Both sides must fold a word the same way**, and for a while they did not. The
gate compares label words against corpus words; the corpus tokenizer strips a
possessive (`Dillard's` → `dillard`) while `normalizeLabel` only ran `singular`
— dropping the final `s` and stranding the apostrophe: `dillard'`. The gate was
asked for a token the corpus could never hold and refused a real name as no
name at all. Measured: two arcs of one earnings story, both labelled "Dillard's
Tariff Refund Earnings", refused for "no grounded name" while `dillard` sat
capitalized in their own coverage. Fixed by giving both callers one fold
(`trimWordPunctuation`) rather than a second regex — the same reason the two
layers share `singular`. Any possessive label was affected, and a plural
possessive ("Workers' Rights" → `workers'`) fails the same way if only `'s` is
stripped.

**The rule** — four clauses, each refusing something the others cannot see:

1. Both labels from the **model**. The bigram fallback named sixteen unrelated
   arcs "Strait Hormuz" — agreement about vocabulary, the anchor rule's
   business.
2. Labels **equal** after casefolding, whitespace collapse and the `singular`
   fold. Stopwords are deliberately *not* elided.
3. The label contains a **grounded proper noun**, pooled over the component's
   whole coverage.
4. Spans **overlap or fall within `STORYLINE_LABEL_MERGE_GRACE_DAYS`**.

Transitive within one label and nowhere else: three arcs chaining A–C–B
collapse together, because C covers the gap and all three carry one name. The
label is the boundary — what makes this bounded where ADR-0009's transitive
token linking was not.

**Measured on the dev archive** (2026-08-17, 258 model-labelled live
storylines):

| label | arcs | grounded names | outcome |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Strait of Hormuz Crisis | 6 | `hormuz` | merged into one 31-group arc |
| Q2 2026 Financial Results | 3 | none | **refused** |

Two shared labels, one merge, one refusal — the refusal being the load-bearing
half. Yield is small only because most of the archive is unlabelled (13,390 of
13,648 live storylines have no model label); under the nightly label chore
every promoted storyline carries one.

### The grace window, measured

`STORYLINE_LABEL_MERGE_GRACE_DAYS` was unmeasured while every arc of the one
qualifying component overlapped every other. The label backfill (2026-08-17,
855 model-labelled live storylines) produced what the knob needed: two shared
labels whose arcs are separated by a real gap, wanting **opposite** answers.

| label | gap | what it is | should merge |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Kyle Shanahan car crash | 12d (Jul 30 → Aug 11) | the crash, then the admission of fault | yes |
| Canada US tariff talks | 19d (Jul 24 → Aug 12) | two rounds of a recurring negotiation | no |

The gap does exactly the work ADR-0010 gives it: a story that stops and
restarts is one storyline; a negotiation that comes back around is not, and the
only thing separating those cases is how long the silence ran.

**14 is the only setting that gets both right** — 7 refuses the Shanahan merge,
20 accepts the tariff merge. The value now rests on data, but on a sample of
two; a third separated component is worth re-reading it against.

Why it went unmeasured so long is worth naming: it needed a *large* labelled
archive, not a clever test. At 258 model labels there were two shared labels
and no gap between any pair of arcs; at 855 there were four, two separated. The
knob was untestable until the backfill ran.

## Operating it

```bash
pnpm label:storylines                  # label promoted storylines that need it
pnpm label:storylines --dry-run        # say which, write nothing
pnpm label:storylines --preview        # read-only quality/cost harness (dies in S9)
pnpm merge:storyline-labels --dry-run  # what the label-agreement merge would do
pnpm merge:storyline-labels            # merge it, and re-name the survivors
```

The dry run prints every shared label with its grounded names and every arc's
span, then the plan and the refusals. Merging has no inverse (ADR-0011 refuses
split), so reading that output is the check — and the refusals are where a
generic model label shows itself.

With no `GEMINI_API_KEY` every path still runs; the model is never asked and
`source` reads `bigram` throughout. Tests never reach the live API: the client
is exercised against recorded payloads in
`fixtures/gemini-label-responses.json`, every shape confirmed against the real
endpoint during the run above.

Latency: ~8s per call end to end over the 20-label run — why the deadline is
20s and why labelling is a background pass, never on a reader's path.
